Schedule B — why are there no dollar amounts?

Because this tool has not earned the right to show you one. Everything above is arithmetic on your own meter readings, which is safe to compute for anybody. Turning it into money means modelling a specific tariff, and the project holds itself to a rule before any dollar figure ships:

No feature that produces a dollar figure ships until the bill engine reproduces real utility bills, from real interval data, within ±$2 per month.

That is met for PG&E — eleven consecutive real statements reproduced, worst error 22 cents. It is not yet met for SDG&E, because no real SDG&E bill has been tested against the engine. So rather than show a confident-looking number the engine has not earned, it shows none. The full method is written up here.

04Obtaining
the file

How to download it

You want the interval usage export — the hour-by-hour readings. The billing or cost summary is a different download and will be turned away here.

PG&E
  1. Sign in at pge.com and open Energy Usage Details.
  2. Find the Green Button control and choose Export usage for a range of days.
  3. Pick the widest range offered — up to 13 months — and download the CSV.
SDG&E
  1. Sign in at sdge.com and open your usage or Green Button tool.
  2. Choose the interval usage export — 15-minute or hourly.
  3. Pick the widest range offered — up to 13 months — and download the CSV.

If you have solar, your file records what you sent to the grid separately. This reads that too, and will say so.

Why 13 months and not a few

California rates change between summer and winter, and so does most households' usage. A comparison built on three months of winter data will mis-rank plans for the summer you never measured.

Both utilities keep 13 months. Export all of it while you can — access usually ends when the account does.
