The short version. This is a free, open-source tool that does arithmetic on your own meter readings. It is not financial, legal or engineering advice, it comes with no warranty, and decisions about your electricity plan, solar or battery remain yours.

What this is

Home Energy Advisor is an independent project that reads the interval usage file your California utility already keeps for you and shows you the shape of your own electricity use. The wider project also contains a bill engine that models published utility tariffs.

It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated on behalf of PG&E, SDG&E, any Community Choice Aggregator, the CPUC, or any solar or battery installer. Utility and company names appear only to identify whose published tariffs are being described.

Not advice

Nothing here is financial, legal, tax or engineering advice, and using the tool creates no professional relationship of any kind. Choosing a rate schedule, buying solar or storage, or switching generation supplier are consequential decisions that depend on facts this tool does not know about you. Verify anything that matters against your own bills and your utility before acting on it.

Accuracy, and its limits

The project holds itself to a published accuracy standard and states plainly where that standard has and has not been met — see the methodology. That is a genuine commitment, and it is still not a guarantee. In particular:

  • Tariffs change. Rates in the engine are transcribed from public regulatory filings that were current when they were read. California rates change mid-year, and a figure can go stale without notice.
  • Your file may be incomplete. Utility exports contain gaps, estimated readings and clock-change artefacts. The tool reports what it finds, but it can only work with what your utility gave you.
  • Analysis is not a bill. Only your utility can tell you what you owe. Where the engine has not reproduced real bills for a utility, this tool deliberately shows you no dollar figure at all rather than a confident-looking guess.
  • Errors are possible. The source is public specifically so that mistakes can be found and reported. If you find one, please open an issue.
